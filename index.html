Federal property disposition

Property bought with federal grant funds (CSP, ESSA titles, IDEA, ESSER) follows a different track from state-funded property. The handbook's page 70–71 process and the forms-folder process sheet, reconciled.

The process (Department of Grant Compliance and Administration)

  1. Inventory by program. Identify every item bought with federal funds, the dollar amount, and the federal program the funds came from. Build the Master Federal Inventory List from the Directives I equipment inventory (state / federal / local lots).
  2. Submit the inventory list to TEA and ask Federal Compliance for access to the EDGAR Connect WorkApp. Contact: Nick Davis.
  3. Classify under 2 CFR 200.1. Equipment (vehicles, long-lived items) is listed per item at fair market value. Supplies (desks, PE items) may be valued in aggregate.
  4. Check the threshold. Use the LEA's local capitalization policy if lower; otherwise the federal $5,000 per-unit line. Over $5,000 and under $5,000 use different WorkApp forms.
  5. File the WorkApp form first. Filing constitutes "requesting instructions" for disposition. Approval (Federal Grants Division) is tracked in SmartSheet; the target is 45 days or less. Do not sell or give anything away before approval.
  6. Over $5,000: the superintendent for closure sells the item and remits proceeds to TEA for refund to the federal government.
  7. Under $5,000: distribute per local policy with no refund, or if no policy: (1) A/B-rated charters, (2) other charters, (3) ISDs via an ESC posting, (4) local non-profits.
  8. Acquisition by another charter: inventory all assets, flag the federal ones, and submit a WorkApp transfer request instead of steps 6–7.

Rules TEA states plainly

  • All federal property is sold and proceeds remitted to the federal government; it cannot be given away like state-funded property.
  • Forms come before buyers. The closed charter does not need to know who will receive the property to file.
  • Cannot be rolled into a real-property sale even if the buyer pays valuation for it.
  • Unsellable items: re-evaluate fair market value; if now under $5,000, file the under-threshold form and document the failed sale attempt.
  • Landlord wants it out? The LEA warehouses the items until disposition is approved.
  • Lienholders cannot be paid from proceeds of federally purchased property. Money owed to the federal government takes precedence.
  • Residual supplies over $5,000 aggregate at closeout: retain for other federal awards or sell, compensating USDE for its share (2 CFR 200.314).
  • Grants go on a global hold and the charter is designated short-term high risk at revocation; TEA sends the conservator instructions for accessing grant funds. Name the TEAL authorized official for federal grant applications.
  • Also due: Federal Expenditure Reports (FER), the Annual Performance Report (APR), and Form 269/269a under 34 CFR 80.41 if funds came directly from USDE.

Records: student, staff, and business

Who keeps what after closure, and the chain of custody TEA expects. ESC Region 13 is the commissioner-designated custodian for every closed charter in Texas; the ESC for the charter's region is the temporary hand-off point.

Chain of custody

  1. Within 30 days of notice (best practice): hard copies of student records available for parent pickup: addresses, schedules, transcripts, final report card, classification, discipline, PGP, immunizations, SSN, IEP/504.
  2. Immediately after classes end: update AARs, issue final report cards and diplomas, hand out TAMS access codes for STAAR results, finalize attendance data for TEA's attendance audit.
  3. Within one month of the last day of classes: box and label every student and staff record (charter name, CDN, box x of xx; indexed alphabetically by year), make two flash-drive copies, and have the local ESC sign the Transfer of Physical and Electronic Records form for the boxes and one drive. The conservator keeps the second drive and the signed form.
  4. Within 10 days of closure: deliver the electronic Student Records directory (TREx-format extracts plus scanned attachments) on a flash drive to the conservator or TEA.
  5. Within one month of the last day of classes: written confirmation from the ESC or a contractor that FERPA data was wiped from every computer, before any device is redistributed.
  6. After closure: the conservator transfers the records from the local ESC to ESC Region 13 (electronic records via TREx). Retain the transfer confirmation forms for the closure evidence file.

Stays with the charter holder (reviewed by superintendent and conservator)

  • Board minutes; policy manual; administrative, accounting, and personnel procedures manuals
  • Bank statements with canceled checks; corporate credit card statements and invoices
  • Accounting reports, budgets, journals, ledgers, registers, petty cash reports
  • Independent auditor annual financial reports; IRS forms and support
  • All agreements, contracts, and shared-service arrangements with exhibits and amendments
  • Purchase requisitions and vouchers with invoices and receipts; reimbursement and travel vouchers
  • All teacher schedules; all electronic financial files, including PEIMS submission files
  • Financial records must be kept for audit under applicable retention schedules and remain the charter holder's responsibility; tell TEA where they are stored.

Goes to the ESC

  • All personnel files, service records, teacher certifications, and a chart of all personnel under contract
  • Student attendance records per the Student Attendance Accounting Handbook, and the electronic attendance files needed for PEIMS
  • All TRS forms and supporting documentation
  • Files containing SSNs secured and labeled CONFIDENTIAL

Best practices from the handbook and directives

Guidance TEA states as expectation or lesson learned rather than as a numbered directive.

Taking control of money on day one

  • Written consent before every transaction: agreements, contracts, purchases, payments, and instructional-materials orders. Say so in writing to leadership.
  • Board resolution within 7 days adding the conservator as agent-signatory and removing all other signatories; on the bank card within 3 days after. Unallowable checks written without the conservator's signature can be disputed with the bank.
  • Put leadership on notice that unauthorized payments are recovered from the employee's paycheck and referred to SBEC.
  • Cancel every credit and debit card; stop ACH payments that should no longer run; later consolidate accounts into one.
  • Get usernames and passwords for every financial system, not just view access; ask for organizational charts and system owners at the first onsite meeting.
  • Bank statements for six months and a full transaction listing from placement forward for each account, not a summary.
  • UCC search for security interests attached to state property; disclose nature and collateral with the creating document.
  • Fiduciary withholdings first: IRS, TRS, health insurance. If not current, get total due with penalties, who is owed, and any warrant holds or levies; if current, get a CFO or superintendent affidavit.

Running the closure

  • Directives can be "soft" or dated. If the administration is cooperative and already moving, issue Directives I and II as a general list with minimal deadlines; tighten to set dates when they need it.
  • Failure to meet directive deadlines can trigger a Board of Managers; the superintendent's final paycheck is contingent on completing Directives II.
  • Remind the charter holder it remains the fiduciary for wind-up obligations and must remit excess funds to the State (TEC §§12.107, 12.122, 12.128, 12.1284; Tex. Const. art. III §52(a); AG Op. GA-0252).
  • Expect early-termination fees when cancelling vendor contracts; request final invoices; give the authorizer a copy of the management-company termination.
  • One year is the goal. Use the SmartSheet checklist, upload proof against each action, and organize the SharePoint evidence file by the eight-item Final Closure Graph so TEA can issue the Official Closure Memo.
  • Keep the insurance policy in force in the charter's name under conservator/BOM control until real property is disposed; pay it on time.
  • Secure real property early: change locks, secure vehicles and technology in the most secure rooms, brief local police on trespass, forward mail, keep utilities paid in consultation with TEA, donate food to a TEA-designated non-profit.
  • Notify AskTED only after the last PEIMS submission so the charter is made obsolete at the right moment.

Students, staff, and communications

  • Parent and staff letters need specific dates: last day of instruction or employment, last day to obtain records on site, how to get records from ESC 13 afterward, final paycheck date, TEA's list of nearby schools, and extended-year services.
  • Post the closure notice on the homepage, at every campus entrance, and wherever board notices are posted; leave it up until the Commissioner's closure date.
  • Deliver a full contact database of staff and students to the conservator; TEA may ask for it if the school is slow to notify parents.
  • Answer TEA Student Attendance on which attendance system was used, how teachers took official attendance, and the last day of school; the attendance audit and settle-up payment are closure-graph items 1 and 2.
  • Terminate summer-extending contracts and programs; arrange Student Success Initiative retesting with a nearby ISD or charter; contact TWC about mass claims.
  • Benefits end on the last day of service under COBRA and plan rules; TRS notified with final payment date and service-record verification; consult counsel on HR issues.
  • Textbooks: stack in hallways outside classrooms, alternating every five books, title and count on top. Inventory goes into lots by funding source.

Getting paid and reporting

  • Expense and progress reports by the 5th to the school's SharePoint folder or Stacey Selman; processed to the district or TEA Accounts Payable by the 15th.
  • Quarter-hour increments; 1 hr 30 min is 1.50, 45 min is .75. Emails may be aggregated (ten 6-minute emails = 1 hour). Describe each task.
  • Mileage needs a map matching the miles claimed (Google Maps printed to PDF works; some phone exports do not). One map per repeated route per month.
  • No tips on meals, Ubers, or taxis; hotel receipts must match the dates claimed; postage, tolls, and closure supplies such as thumb drives are "Incidentals".
  • Who pays: the district until its final FSP payment or until closure if funds allow; when TEA Accounts Payable pays, sign page one by hand or attach a scanned signed copy.
  • Progress report structure (exemplar pp. 82–86): placement information, entry conditions, exit criteria goals with goal progress measures, monthly activities, directives log, and board interactions with attendance percentage and meeting length.
  • Attend at least 80% of regular board meetings and of any non-closed workshops, hearings, or committees unless TEA directs otherwise.
